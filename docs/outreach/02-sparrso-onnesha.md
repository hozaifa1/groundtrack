# 02 — SPARRSO and BRAC Onnesha alumni

SPARRSO is the Bangladesh Space Research and Remote Sensing Organization. BRAC Onnesha
was Bangladesh's first CubeSat, deployed from the ISS in 2017 — its team is findable on
LinkedIn and most of them are now in industry or graduate programmes.

These are the only people in the country who have actually operated a spacecraft. A
reply here is worth more than any of the others, and it is also the most local ask.

---

**Subject:** From a DU EEE student — one question about anomaly response on Onnesha

---

Assalamu alaikum <name>,

I'm Hozaifa, final-semester EEE at the University of Dhaka. You worked on <Onnesha /
the ground segment at SPARRSO>, which makes you one of a very small number of people in
Bangladesh who have actually sat on the operations side of a spacecraft.

I've built a tool on an assumption I cannot check by myself, and I would rather have it
corrected than published.

The assumption: a small team writes an anomaly-response procedure at commissioning —
this telemetry signature means that corrective action — and then it is rarely
revalidated against real flight data, because revalidating means replaying months of
telemetry against the current rules and patching whatever it missed. So a new fault
mode appears, and the person on console is reading a document written before it existed.

What I built re-tunes an anomaly detector automatically against labelled NASA telemetry
(SMAP and MSL), with an IBM Granite model writing a plain-language brief for each
detection. Every change the agent proposes is checked against a fixed scorer it cannot
edit, and every attempt is logged including the failures.

**One question, and one sentence is a complete answer:** on Onnesha — or on anything you
have operated — was a stale anomaly procedure a real problem, or is that a student's
guess about a job he has not done?

I am not asking for data or for a meeting. If it is not a real problem I will say so in
the project's own README, because a corrected assumption is more useful to me than a
flattering one.

## Notes for sending

- Find Onnesha alumni on LinkedIn by searching the mission name; the team was small and
  publicly credited. Send a connection note first if there is no email — the same text
  trimmed to the connection-request limit works.
- For SPARRSO, use the published institutional contact and ask to be pointed at whoever
  handles satellite ground operations. Do not guess an individual address.
- Keep the Islamic greeting only where it fits the recipient. It is natural here and
  would be affectation elsewhere.
