# 03 — DU EEE faculty and IEEE contacts

The ask here is **an introduction, not an answer**. Faculty are unlikely to have
operated a spacecraft, but they are one hop from someone who has, and a forwarded email
from a supervisor outperforms a cold one by a wide margin.

Keep it short. This is the one that gets read on a phone between classes.

---

**Subject:** Would you know anyone on the operations side of a satellite programme?

---

Assalamu alaikum Sir/Madam,

I'm Hozaifa, final-semester EEE. I've built a project for the IBM AI Builders Challenge
(space exploration theme) and I need one thing I can't get from the department: fifteen
minutes with somebody who has actually operated a satellite.

The project automatically re-tunes a spacecraft anomaly detector against labelled NASA
telemetry, and writes a plain-language brief for each detection. The technical side is
done and measurable. What I cannot verify alone is whether the problem I picked is a
real problem for the people I claim to be helping — small mission-ops teams whose
anomaly runbook goes stale because nobody has time to revalidate it.

**Would you know anyone in aerospace, remote sensing, ground-station work, or at
SPARRSO whom I could ask one question?** The question is: "would this have saved you
time?" — that's the entire ask, and one sentence answers it.

If it's easier, I'm happy to send you a two-minute recording you can forward as-is.

Thank you for your time.

Hozaifa
<student ID / section>
<email>

---

## Notes for sending

- Send to supervisors and to anyone in the department with remote-sensing, RF, or
  communications interests before broadcasting wider.
- IEEE student-branch mailing lists are a reasonable second wave, but personal email
  first — a list post is easy to ignore and cannot be forwarded with a recommendation.
- If a faculty member offers to forward it, send them the recording link within the
  hour. That offer expires.
- Do not attach the repository. A link is enough and an attachment triggers filters.
